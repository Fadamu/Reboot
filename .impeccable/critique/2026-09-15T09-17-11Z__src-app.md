## Design Specificity Verdict

REBOOT is partly authored, not yet fully coherent. The recovery mission—connecting a failed result, materials, topics, practice, performance, and a retake—is product-specific and differentiated. Much of the dashboard and supporting interface still uses interchangeable dark-card SaaS patterns. The underlying visual discipline is promising, but the app lacks a shared shell and a mature semantic system that would make it feel like one premium academic operating system.

The deterministic scan returned one warning at `src/app/globals.css:45`: `font-family: Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif;` with the `overused-font` rule. This is low-confidence: the detector rule documentation does not list Arial among its intended matches. It is nevertheless evidence that the typography needs a deliberate product decision rather than a default system fallback.

## Overall Impression

The largest opportunity is to turn the app from a collection of capable pages into a continuous, evidence-led academic recovery system: one student record, one daily priority, and a visible path from setback through readiness to verified clearance.

## What's Working

- The recovery mission is REBOOT's clearest original experience. It combines result, study activity, material, topic, quiz, and performance data into a meaningful academic narrative.
- The study loop is grounded in student-owned material: notes can become topics, focused study, and quizzes rather than generic content.
- The restrained near-black surfaces, rounded cards, and sharp numeric emphasis provide a credible serious starting point.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Fragmented, desktop-only wayfinding

The dashboard sidebar disappears below `lg`, while inner routes lack a consistent app shell or current-location structure. Students can become stranded after entering a task.

**Fix:** Establish one responsive authenticated shell with a desktop rail, labeled mobile navigation, active route state, and contextual course/recovery breadcrumbs.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`, then `$impeccable adapt`.

### [P1] The recovery promise lacks an explicit closure

“Clear it” remains incomplete and the recovery mission does not hand students clearly into recording a retake result. This breaks the product's most important story at its final step.

**Fix:** Create a final recovery state with a dated retake target, a plain definition of cleared, a “Record retake result” action, and a reconciled outcome state.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`, `$impeccable harden`.

### [P1] High-stakes status and failure feedback is unreliable

Dashboard loading failures can look like factual zero metrics. Notes and Planner use blocking alerts, and validation is rarely local or actionable.

**Fix:** Design inline status/live-region components; distinguish no data from failed loading; preserve inputs; provide Retry; validate time ordering and file requirements before submission.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`, `$impeccable clarify`.

### [P2] Recovery language over-militarizes academic setbacks

“Battlefield,” “attack,” and “prove it” risk heightening shame after a failed course, conflicting with a serious, trustworthy academic-OS position.

**Fix:** Use evidence-led terms such as “Recovery queue,” “focus areas,” “demonstrate readiness,” “retake plan,” and “next academic action.”

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`, `$impeccable quieter`.

### [P2] Supporting workflows are dense and slow for repeated work

Notes combines two entry modes in a long form, repeat data entry lacks fast paths, and dashboard card density dilutes the day’s next action.

**Fix:** Stage material input with a mode switch; promote one dashboard priority; add bulk/import, duplicate, and recent-course paths where appropriate.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`, `$impeccable layout`.

## Cognitive Load

High load is driven by hidden mobile navigation, a multi-screen recovery loop without persistent workflow context, dense Notes decisions, inconsistent feedback patterns, competing dashboard card groups, and jargon that requires students to translate the metaphor before acting.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Alex (power user):** Results, courses, notes, and sessions are entered one at a time. There are no shortcuts, batch paths, duplicate actions, or quick capture.
- **Sam (accessibility-dependent):** Navigation disappears at mobile breakpoints; alerts and status changes lack designed live announcements; focus visibility relies on thin border treatments; status colors carry substantial semantic weight.
- **Casey (distracted mobile user):** App navigation disappears on the dashboard, primary actions often sit below long content, and locally held study/quiz state may be lost on refresh or interruption.

## Recommended Direction

Create a premium, evidence-led academic operating system: a quiet ink/charcoal foundation; red only for risk or required attention; amber for active recovery; green only for verified progress. Replace combat framing with “academic record → recovery plan → evidence of readiness → retake outcome.” Make the dashboard a daily decision surface with one clear next action; use a shared responsive shell and data provenance such as “based on 3 completed sessions” or “last updated.”

## Questions to Consider

- Does recovery need to feel combative, or should it feel clinically supportive and evidence-led?
- Which deserves the first redesign investment: cross-app navigation, the recovery end-state, or high-stakes feedback?
- Should the dashboard optimize for one recommended next action or remain an overview-first workspace?
